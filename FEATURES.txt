================================================================================
                           REVIEWPULSE — FEATURES GUIDE
              What every feature means and what insight it gives you
================================================================================

1. WHAT THE APPLICATION DOES (one paragraph)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
ReviewPulse takes a Flipkart product URL, scrapes every customer review (up to
~300 per product, the Flipkart cap), runs nine layers of analysis on the text,
and shows the result as a premium dashboard. Instead of forcing you to read
hundreds of reviews to understand a product, it surfaces what people actually
think about specific features (sound, battery, ANC, mic, build, etc.), groups
recurring complaints into clusters, tracks how sentiment changed month by month,
and lets you filter the raw reviews by any combination of dimensions.


2. THE PIPELINE — WHAT HAPPENS BEHIND THE SCENES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
When you paste a URL and hit Analyze, five stages run in order. The loader UI
shows each stage as it executes.

  Stage 1 — Scrape
    Pulls every review page from Flipkart using Chrome TLS impersonation
    (curl_cffi) to bypass anti-bot blocks. Reviews come from a hidden JSON
    state on the page (window.__INITIAL_STATE__), not from CSS selectors —
    that makes the scraper robust to Flipkart redesigns. Captures author,
    rating, title, full comment text, date, helpful count, verified flag,
    city, and state.

  Stage 2 — Enrich + sentiment
    For every review: computes VADER sentiment (compound score from −1 to +1),
    classifies it as positive / neutral / negative, parses the date (handles
    both "Feb, 2024" and "10 months ago" formats), counts words and
    characters, detects emojis, detects multiline structure, detects
    feature-breakdown patterns ("Sound: 9/10\nBattery: 10/10"), flags
    rating-vs-sentiment mismatches, and flags reviews that talk mostly
    about delivery/returns instead of the product.

  Stage 3 — Aspect analysis (ABSA)
    Splits each review into sentences. For every sentence, checks whether
    it mentions one of nine product aspects (Sound, ANC, Battery, Mic,
    Comfort, Connectivity, Build, Controls, Value). Each aspect-mentioning
    sentence gets its own sentiment score. The system aggregates per
    aspect: how many reviews mentioned it, how many sentences were
    positive vs negative, and which aspects are "polarizing" (high mix
    of positive AND negative). This is what powers the radar chart and
    the per-aspect bars.

  Stage 4 — Cluster complaints
    Collects only the negative-leaning sentences (from low-rated reviews
    or from negatively-toned sentences in any review). Vectorizes them
    with TF-IDF (1-grams and 2-grams), then runs KMeans (k=5) to group
    similar complaints together. Each resulting cluster gets a label
    from its top distinguishing terms, an exemplar quote, the average
    rating of the source reviews, and a list of review IDs. This is
    what brands pay $$$ for: discovering recurring defect signatures.

  Stage 5 — Timeseries + Geo + Pros/Cons
    Buckets reviews by month for the timeline chart. Computes per-state
    aggregates for the geography chart. Mines pros and cons by splitting
    reviews on contrastive conjunctions ("but", "however", "although") —
    in a 4★/5★ review, the part AFTER "but" is typically a complaint;
    in a 1★/2★ review it is typically the redeeming feature.


3. THE THREE UI STATES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  HERO STATE
    What you see first. Centered title, single accent gradient backdrop,
    a search bar with a URL prefix tag, page-count selector, and four
    micro-stats (9 aspects, 5 clusters, 25 months, 300 reviews). The
    purpose is clarity: paste URL, choose how many pages to scrape,
    click Analyze.

  LOADER STATE
    A shimmer bar plus five pipeline steps. Each step lights up
    (active = pulsing dot) then completes (green checkmark) as the
    backend works through it. Communicates progress without spinning
    forever — you can see exactly which stage is running.

  DASHBOARD STATE
    Two-column layout: 220px sidebar nav on the left, main bento-grid
    content area on the right. The sidebar has scroll-spy: as you scroll,
    the active section is highlighted automatically. A "New analysis"
    button at the bottom of the sidebar takes you back to the hero.


4. SECTION-BY-SECTION FEATURE GUIDE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

================================================================================
SECTION: OVERVIEW
================================================================================
The top-level snapshot. What you should look at before drilling in.

  KPI #1 — Reviews
    The total number of reviews scraped (max 300 — Flipkart's hard limit).
    Footer shows what % were verified buyers.

  KPI #2 — Avg rating
    Mean of all star ratings. The footer shows what % gave 5 stars.
    A high average with most reviews at 5★ is a positive review skew —
    common on Flipkart and useful to know before trusting the number.

  KPI #3 — Positive %
    What fraction of reviews have positive VADER sentiment (compound > 0.1).
    This is text-based, not star-based. A 5★ review with negative content
    counts as negative here. So this number can disagree with the rating
    average — that's a signal, not a bug.

  KPI #4 — Negative %
    The mirror of KPI #3 — fraction with compound < −0.1. The most
    important number for spotting hidden product problems.

  KPI #5 — Verified %
    What fraction were confirmed Flipkart purchases. Flipkart already
    filters most spam, so on this platform it's typically 100%, but
    if it drops, trust the data less.

  KPI #6 — Mismatches (rating ↔ tone)
    The count of reviews where the star rating disagrees with the text
    sentiment. Two patterns to watch for:
      • 5★ but negative text → could be sarcasm, or could be someone
        who liked the product but is complaining about delivery.
      • 1★ but positive text → could be a polite complainer who
        described the issue calmly. These are usually high-quality
        critical reviews — worth reading.
    A high mismatch count means VADER is being fooled OR people are
    expressing complaints in unexpected ways.

  CHART — Sentiment over time (8 cols)
    Line chart spanning every month with reviews. Two lines:
      • Solid line = average rating per month (left axis, 1–5 scale)
      • Dashed line = average sentiment per month (right axis, −1 to +1)
    Watch for: divergence between the two lines (rating stays high
    but sentiment drops = something is wrong but stars don't show it),
    sudden volume spikes (a sale or a quality event), gradual
    deterioration (early adopters loved it, later buyers don't).

  CHART — Ratings (4 cols)
    Horizontal bar chart of how many reviews gave each star rating.
    Color-coded green (5/4) → yellow (3) → red (2/1). Tells you the
    shape of the distribution at a glance — heavy 5★ skew vs U-shaped
    polarization.


================================================================================
SECTION: ASPECTS — the headline analytical feature
================================================================================
Star ratings tell you "people like this product overall." Aspect-Based
Sentiment Analysis tells you "people love the sound BUT think the mic is
weak." That second insight is what makes review intelligence valuable.

The nine aspects analyzed:

  • Sound          — Audio quality, bass, treble, clarity, volume
  • ANC            — Active noise cancellation effectiveness
  • Battery        — Playback time, charging, drainage
  • Mic            — Call quality, microphone clarity
  • Comfort        — Fit, ear-tip pressure, headache complaints
  • Connectivity   — Bluetooth pairing, multipoint, lag, latency
  • Build          — Design, case, materials, durability
  • Controls       — Touch, taps, gestures, buttons
  • Value          — Price, money, worth, expensive/cheap

  CHART — Aspect ratings (radar/spider, 5 cols)
    Each axis is one of the nine aspects. The shape of the polygon
    reveals the product's strengths and weaknesses: a balanced
    pentagon = consistently good; a sharp dent on one axis = a known
    weak point; a stretched corner = a standout strength.

  CHART — Per-aspect breakdown (bars, 7 cols)
    For each aspect, a horizontal stacked bar showing the SENTENCE-LEVEL
    sentiment split (positive / neutral / negative). On the right:
    the average star rating of reviews mentioning that aspect, plus
    the mention count. This is denser than the spider — it tells you
    not just "ANC is 4.36★" but "of 91 ANC sentences, 61 are positive,
    20 neutral, 10 negative."

    Polarizing tag: an aspect gets a "POLARIZING" badge if at least
    30% of its sentences are positive AND at least 20% are negative.
    These are the divisive features — same product, opposite
    experiences. Worth digging into.


================================================================================
SECTION: THEMES — what people praise and complain about
================================================================================

  CHIP CLOUD — Pros (6 cols, green chips)
    Auto-extracted phrases that appear on the POSITIVE side of "but"
    splits, plus phrases from highly-rated reviews. Each chip shows the
    phrase and how many reviews used it. These are what customers
    actually love, in their own words.

  CHIP CLOUD — Cons (6 cols, red chips)
    Same logic, mirrored — phrases from the NEGATIVE side of "but"
    splits and from low-rated reviews. If the same phrase appears in
    both pros and cons, that's correctly captured polarization (some
    people love what others hate).

  COMPLAINT CLUSTERS (full-width grid of cards)
    The most actionable feature in the entire dashboard. Each card
    represents a recurring negative theme that the algorithm
    discovered on its own (no hand-coded rules).

    Card contents:
      • Label — the top distinguishing terms for the cluster
      • Avg rating — average star rating of source reviews (always
        low, since these are negative-leaning)
      • Exemplar quote — the most representative actual review
        sentence in the cluster
      • Footer — total reviews and total sentences in the cluster

    What to read for: defects ("battery drains fast"), service
    issues ("open-box delivery damaged"), feature gaps ("no
    multipoint switching"), comfort problems ("hurts after an hour").
    Each cluster typically maps to one specific real-world issue.


================================================================================
SECTION: GEOGRAPHY
================================================================================
Where the reviews come from, and how location affects sentiment.

  CHART — By state (horizontal bars, 7 cols)
    States ranked by review count (top 12 shown). Each bar's length is
    the average rating, color-coded green (≥4.5) → yellow (≥3.5) → red.
    Hover shows count and exact rating. What this surfaces: regional
    sentiment differences. If one state's average is significantly
    lower, it might indicate a regional logistics problem, fake-review
    farms, or product compatibility issues with local conditions.

  RANK LIST — Cities (5 cols)
    Top 10 cities by review count, mono-formatted with rank numbers.
    Useful for understanding the product's geographic concentration.


================================================================================
SECTION: TIMELINE
================================================================================

  CHART — Monthly review activity (full width, stacked bars)
    Each bar is one month. Three stacked segments per bar:
      • Green = positive reviews
      • Gray = neutral
      • Red = negative
    Bar height = total review volume that month. Watch for: review
    volume spikes (a sale event or viral mention), growing red
    segments over time (quality declining), or a sudden shift in
    the green:red ratio (something changed about the product).

    Note: the dates were rescued from Flipkart's mixed format —
    "Feb, 2024" reviews and "10 months ago" reviews are both
    plotted on the same axis using the timeline parser.


================================================================================
SECTION: REVIEWS — filterable raw data
================================================================================
Where you go to read actual customer text after the dashboards have
told you what to look for.

  FILTER BAR
    • Rating filter   — show only 1★ / 2★ / 3★ / 4★ / 5★
    • Sentiment filter — show only positive / neutral / negative
    • Aspect filter    — show only reviews mentioning sound, ANC, etc.
    • Length filter    — short (<20 chars) / medium (20–200) / long (200+)
    • Search box       — keyword search in author + title + comment
    • Filter count     — live tally of matches

    Filters are pre-computed inverted indexes in the JSON: the JS
    intersects ID lists at O(1) per filter, so combining multiple
    filters is instant even on large datasets.

  REVIEW CARDS (sorted by helpful count, top 50 shown)
    Each card shows:
      • Avatar (initials), author name, location, date
      • Stars (mono-formatted, dots for missing stars)
      • Sentiment chip (POS / NEU / NEG, color-coded)
      • Title (the canned Flipkart phrase or custom title)
      • Full comment text (preserves newlines, wraps long lines)
      • Footer flags:
          – 👍 N         = helpful vote count
          – Nw           = word count
          – aspect tags  = which aspects this review mentions
          – ⚠ mismatch  = star rating disagrees with text sentiment
          – 📦 logistics = review centers on delivery/returns


5. SPECIAL FLAGS — WHAT THEY MEAN AND WHY THEY MATTER
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  MISMATCH (⚠)
    The star rating disagrees with the text sentiment. Two cases:
      • 4★ or 5★ rating with negative text → user gave a high rating
        out of habit but the text reveals a problem. Frequently
        these are Flipkart's canned title issue (titled "Just wow!"
        but content says "Worst delivery service").
      • 1★ or 2★ rating with positive text → user is a polite
        complainer. These are usually the highest-quality critical
        reviews because the person took time to explain calmly.

  LOGISTICS (📦)
    The review text mentions delivery, return, refund, warranty,
    open-box, courier, or shipping. This separates supply-chain
    pain from product pain. A product with 30% logistics-flagged
    reviews has a fulfillment issue, not a product issue.

  POLARIZING (aspect badge)
    An aspect where users sharply disagree — at least 30% positive
    and at least 20% negative sentences. These features are
    inconsistent across the user base. ANC is often polarizing on
    earbuds because effectiveness depends on ear-tip fit.

  ANONYMOUS
    Reviews from users displaying as "Flipkart Customer" rather
    than a real name. Typically ~12% of any product's reviews.
    Not necessarily fake, but worth noting.

  HAS_FEATURE_BREAKDOWN
    Reviews that contain mini-ratings like "Sound: 9/10\nBattery: 10/10".
    These are the most engaged reviews. The 476-helpful exemplar in
    the OnePlus Buds 3 dataset uses this format — readers reward
    structured criticism with helpful votes.


6. WHAT TO LOOK FOR — SAMPLE INSIGHT WORKFLOW
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
A typical session for a buying decision:

  1) OVERVIEW: Is the avg rating real or inflated by 5★ skew? Check
     the rating chart — if 70%+ are 5★, the average is misleading.
     Also check the mismatch count — if it's >5%, the average rating
     is hiding text-based dissatisfaction.

  2) ASPECTS: Look at the spider chart for the weakest axis. That
     is the product's known weakness. Then confirm in the per-aspect
     bars: how many sentences are negative vs positive on that axis?

  3) THEMES → CLUSTERS: Read the exemplar quote on each cluster.
     These are the recurring complaints. If a cluster maps to
     something you care about (battery, mic, ANC), drill in.

  4) THEMES → CONS: The chip cloud surfaces the top complaint
     phrases. Compare with the cluster labels — they should
     overlap and reinforce each other.

  5) TIMELINE: Has sentiment changed over time? A product that
     was 4.7★ in 2024 but 4.0★ in 2026 is degrading.

  6) REVIEWS: Filter to your specific concern (e.g., aspect = mic,
     sentiment = negative). Read the top-helpful matching reviews.
     These are the most-validated specific complaints.


7. THE DATA SCHEMA (v2.0) — WHAT GETS STORED
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The /analyze endpoint returns a JSON with nine top-level sections:

  meta        — schema version, scrape time, review count, product URL
  aggregates  — totals: rating distribution, sentiment counts, top cities,
                top states, length buckets, mismatch count, etc.
  aspects     — per-aspect ABSA: rating, mentions, pos/neu/neg sentence
                counts, polarizing flag, list of source review IDs
  summary     — pros and cons (heuristic phrase mining)
  clusters    — complaint clusters (label, exemplar, size, avg rating, IDs)
  timeseries  — monthly buckets (month, n, avg rating, avg sentiment,
                positive count, negative count)
  geo         — per-state aggregates (count, avg rating, sentiment split)
  filters     — inverted indexes by_rating, by_sentiment, by_aspect,
                by_cluster, by_city, by_state, by_year, by_length_bucket,
                with_emoji, multiline, anonymous, mismatch, logistics
  reviews     — every review keyed by UUID, fully enriched with author,
                rating, title (text + category), comment (text + 8
                computed flags), date (raw + iso + days_ago + format),
                engagement (helpful_count), analysis (sentiment_score,
                sentiment_label, mismatch flag), aspect_mentions
                (per-sentence aspect hits), aspects_present (set of
                aspects this review mentions)


8. WHAT MAKES THE DESIGN PREMIUM
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Compared to a typical free dashboard template, the visual design follows
five rules borrowed from products like Linear, Vercel, and Stripe:

  • One accent color only (Linear indigo #5E6AD2). No rainbow palettes.
  • Numbers are in JetBrains Mono with tabular-nums so digits don't
    jiggle when values update. This single choice moves a dashboard
    up two tiers in perceived quality.
  • Cards use 1px borders, no drop shadows. Shadows feel cheap.
  • Bento grid (asymmetric column spans of 2/4/5/6/7/8/12) instead of
    uniform 4×N grids. Creates visual rhythm.
  • 8px card radius, 6px buttons, 4px inputs — consistent ratio.
  • Charts: no vertical gridlines, horizontal at 4% opacity, 2px line
    strokes, gradient area fills 16%→0%, mono tooltips with dark bg.
  • 150ms ease transitions only. No bouncing, no 300ms eases.
  • Skeleton shimmer instead of spinners.
